# Assessment Scope

## 1. Purpose

This document defines the boundaries, objectives, and limitations of the simulated ISO/IEC 27001:2022 Annex A control gap assessment for Meridian Digital Services.

The purpose is to identify potential information security control gaps, document preliminary risks, and recommend improvements as part of a GRC portfolio project.

## 2. Organization Profile

- **Organization:** Meridian Digital Services
- **Industry:** Software and IT Services
- **Location:** United States
- **Employees:** Approximately 150
- **Technology Environment:** Microsoft 365, cloud-hosted applications, employee laptops, and third-party service providers.

**Note:** Meridian Digital Services is a fictional organization created for this portfolio project. The organization profile and assessment scenarios are illustrative.

## 3. Assessment Boundaries

The assessment covers 30 selected controls from ISO/IEC 27001:2022 Annex A across four control categories:

- Organizational controls
- People controls
- Physical controls
- Technological controls

The selected controls are documented in `docs/selected-controls.md` and assessed in `assessment/iso27001_gap_assessment.csv`.

The project also includes:

- A Statement of Applicability (SoA) excerpt covering the selected controls.
- An evidence tracking matrix for proposed evidence requests and follow-up.
- Preliminary mapping of selected controls to NIST CSF 2.0.
- A remediation plan with suggested actions, owners, priorities, and timelines.
- An assessment findings report and executive summary.

The SoA excerpt is limited to the 30 selected controls. It is not a complete Statement of Applicability for all Annex A controls.

## 4. Assessment Objectives

The assessment aims to:

1. Evaluate the simulated implementation status of selected information security controls.
2. Identify and document potential control gaps.
3. Record control applicability and preliminary treatment decisions in the SoA excerpt.
4. Identify evidence needed to support control assessments and track outstanding requests.
5. Assign preliminary qualitative risk ratings to identified gaps.
6. Map selected ISO/IEC 27001 controls to relevant NIST CSF 2.0 outcomes.
7. Recommend corrective actions and proposed remediation timelines.
8. Present findings in a format suitable for management review.

## 5. Assessment Approach

The project uses a simulated review of hypothetical organizational policies, procedures, records, and other evidence descriptions.

Each selected control is assigned an implementation status based on the constructed scenario and available assessment information. Potential gaps, evidence deficiencies, and recommendations are recorded in the assessment files.

Evidence requests are tracked separately. Requests that have not been received or validated remain outstanding; they are not treated as independently verified evidence.

The assessment methodology is documented in `docs/assessment-methodology.md`.

## 6. Exclusions

The following activities are outside the scope of this portfolio project:

- Assessing all 93 ISO/IEC 27001:2022 Annex A controls.
- Conducting an audit of a real organization or its operational systems.
- Performing technical penetration testing, vulnerability scanning, or configuration testing.
- Independently verifying organizational records or control effectiveness.
- Conducting a formal certification audit or issuing a certification decision.
- Providing legal advice or confirming compliance with every applicable law or regulation.

## 7. Limitations

This is a simulated portfolio assessment based on a fictional organization. The control statuses, risk ratings, applicability decisions, and remediation recommendations are illustrative and have not been validated through an independent review of a real operating environment.

The project does not establish ISO/IEC 27001 conformity or certification readiness. Evidence validation, management review, and final quality assurance would be required before similar findings could support real organizational decisions.
